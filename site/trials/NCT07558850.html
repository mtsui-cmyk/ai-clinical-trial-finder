Exploratory Clinical Study of Anti-CD19/BCMA Universal CAR-T Cell Injection for the Treatment of Refractory Autoimmune Diseases

Recruiting No standard drug phase listed NCT07558850 AI explained

This pilot study is testing a type of cell therapy called anti-CD19/BCMA-UCAR-T cells in people with certain autoimmune diseases. The study will enroll about 36 to 72 participants.

Research context

Study of Anti-CD19/BCMA CAR-T Cells for Autoimmune Diseases

Researchers are studying whether anti-CD19/BCMA-UCAR-T cells can be safe and associated with the study outcome for treating autoimmune diseases such as dermatomyositis, rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and systemic sclerosis.

What terms may be confusing?

  • SLE: systemic lupus erythematosus, the medical name for lupus in many registry records.
  • CAR-T / cell therapy: immune-cell therapy being studied in some records; this page does not say it is suitable for anyone.
  • CD19: an immune-cell marker that some cell therapy and antibody studies target.
  • BCMA: an immune-cell target that may appear in some cell therapy or antibody research.
  • Biologic / antibody: an immune-targeting research intervention category.

Source text

Official summary excerpt

A single arm, open-label pilot study is designed to determine the safety and effectiveness of anti-CD19/BCMA-UCAR-T cells in patients with autoimmune diseases. 36-72 patients are planned to be enrolled in the dose-escalation trial.
